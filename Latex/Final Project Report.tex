\section{Data and Metrics Overview}
\subsection{Dataset}
Season-level NBA player data covering demographics, full-season box score totals, and advanced metrics. Core totals include games, minutes, points, rebounds, assists, and shooting percentages; advanced metrics summarize win contribution and on-court impact.

\subsection{Advanced Metrics (Focus)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{WS and WS/48}: Win Shares (WS) measure total wins a player adds, split into offensive (OWS) and defensive (DWS) parts. WS/48 scales WS by playing time, representing wins added if the player maintained the same efficiency over 48 minutes.
    \item \textbf{BPM and VORP}: Box Plus/Minus (BPM) estimates points per 100 possessions a player adds relative to average, using a regression over full box score stats. Value Over Replacement Player (VORP) shifts BPM by a constant baseline (around --2) to express net impact versus a replacement-level player.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data Processing}
\subsection{Cleaning}
Removed players who never appeared in a game. Filled numeric missing values with 0 and set missing \texttt{college} to \texttt{International}. To avoid overfitting in dimensionality reduction while keeping these metrics for comparison, advanced metric columns were dropped during PCA and UMAP. Per-game stats were excluded as they are deterministic from totals and introduce collinearity with different units; totals were retained for consistency.

\subsection{Feature Engineering and Selection}
Counted player production by college, kept the top 20 sources (including International) as one-hot categories, grouped the rest into \texttt{Others}; standardized draft year and one-hot encoded draft team. These categorical features performed poorly in PCA/UMAP, so the final reduction used only numeric core features: \texttt{games}, \texttt{minutes\_played}, \texttt{points}, \texttt{total\_rebounds}, \texttt{assists}, \texttt{field\_goal\_percentage}, \texttt{3\_point\_percentage}, \texttt{free\_throw\_percentage}.

\section{Dimensionality Reduction and EDA}
\subsection{PCA Results}
Plotted cumulative explained variance and component-wise variance bars. The first two principal components explain about 74\% of total variance: $PC_1$ captures overall counting volume (low weight on shooting accuracy), while $PC_2$ is driven almost entirely by three-point and free-throw percentages and is weakly or negatively related to the other stats. We present the numeric-only PCA version because including advanced metrics produced unstable structure.

In 2D PCA space we color points by quartiles of WS, WS/48, BPM, and VORP (red = lowest quartile, dark green = highest) and highlight LeBron James, Tim Duncan, Kobe Bryant, Stephen Curry, and Kyrie Irving. Observations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]

    \item \textbf{WS/48 more diffuse}: Lowest quartile remains clustered, but middle and upper quartiles intermingle, showing that efficient low-usage players can still add wins; teams are not built solely by stacking high-usage stars.

\section{Model Setup}
\subsection{Model Overview}

We propose a \emph{Bayesian Dirichlet--Gaussian admixture model}, which can be viewed as a
\emph{Gaussian mixed-membership model} with Dirichlet-distributed latent proportions.
From a probabilistic modeling perspective, our approach is closely related to
Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA)--style mixed-membership models, Bayesian Gaussian mixture
models, and grade-of-membership (GoM) models for multivariate continuous data.

Unlike standard Gaussian mixture models, which enforce a single cluster assignment per
observation, our model allows each player to be represented as a \emph{convex combination}
of multiple latent ``styles.'' This design reflects the continuous and overlapping nature
of player archetypes in professional basketball, where individuals often exhibit hybrid
roles rather than belonging to a single discrete category.

\vspace{0.5em}

\subsection{Generative Model}

Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times P}$ denote the standardized feature matrix, where each row
corresponds to a player and each column corresponds to a performance feature.
For a fixed number of latent styles $K$, the generative process is defined as:
\begin{align}
\theta_n &\sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\boldsymbol{\alpha}), \quad n = 1, \dots, N, \\
\beta_{k,p} &\sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau_p), 
\quad k = 1, \dots, K,\; p = 1, \dots, P, \\
\sigma_p &\sim \mathrm{Half\text{-}Cauchy}(\tau_\sigma), \\
X_{n,p} \mid \theta_n, \beta, \sigma_p 
&\sim \mathcal{N}\!\left(
\sum_{k=1}^{K} \theta_{n,k}\,\beta_{k,p},\; \sigma_p
\right).
\end{align}

Here, $\theta_n \in \Delta^{K-1}$ represents the style composition of player $n$,
lying on the probability simplex.
The matrix $\beta \in \mathbb{R}^{K \times P}$ encodes latent style-specific feature
profiles, while $\sigma_p$ captures feature-specific noise and heteroskedasticity.

\vspace{0.5em}

\subsection{Prior Design and Modeling Motivation}

\paragraph{Dirichlet prior on latent proportions.}
The Dirichlet prior on $\theta_n$ provides a natural inductive bias for mixed-membership
modeling, enforcing non-negativity and unit-sum constraints on style proportions.
To mitigate label symmetry and posterior collapse toward uniform assignments, we employ a
\emph{non-symmetric Dirichlet prior}
\[
\boldsymbol{\alpha} = \alpha \cdot \mathbf{w},
\]
where $\mathbf{w}$ is a positive, non-uniform weight vector.
This asymmetry encourages distinct latent roles while preserving model flexibility.

\paragraph{Hierarchical priors on style loadings.}
Rather than imposing a single global variance on all feature loadings, we introduce
group-specific scale parameters for different types of features (e.g., counting statistics,
shooting efficiency metrics, and advanced performance indicators).
This hierarchical structure reflects domain knowledge about heterogeneous feature variability
and improves robustness across disparate measurement scales.

\paragraph{Noise scale prior.}
Feature-specific noise parameters $\sigma_p$ are assigned Half-Cauchy priors, which are
weakly informative and heavy-tailed.
This choice allows the model to accommodate residual outliers while avoiding excessive
regularization of observation noise.

Overall, these priors encode soft structural assumptions---continuous style mixtures,
heterogeneous feature variability, and asymmetric latent roles---while remaining
sufficiently flexible for data-driven unsupervised discovery.

\section{Variational Inference and Training}

Exact posterior inference for this model is intractable due to the coupling between
$\theta$, $\beta$, and $\sigma$.
We therefore adopt stochastic variational inference (SVI) with a mean-field variational
family to approximate the true posterior distribution.

Let $z = (\theta, \beta, \sigma)$ denote all latent variables.
We approximate $p(z \mid X)$ with a variational distribution $q_\phi(z)$ and maximize the
evidence lower bound (ELBO):
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\phi)
=
\mathbb{E}_{q_\phi(z)}\!\left[\log p(X \mid z)\right]
-
\mathrm{KL}\!\left(q_\phi(z)\;\|\;p(z)\right).
\end{equation}

\section{Tuning and Model Criterion.}
We adopt a staged hyperparameter tuning strategy for the generative style-topic model rather than an exhaustive grid search. 
The candidate configuration space includes both discrete structural parameters and continuous scale parameters. 
The number of latent styles is fixed to $K \in \{3,5,7\}$, while the Dirichlet concentration parameter is fixed to $\alpha = 0.5$. 
To avoid complete prior symmetry across latent styles and improve identifiability, the Dirichlet parameter is multiplied by a fixed weight vector that is linearly spaced between $0.7$ and $1.3$ and normalized to have unit mean; this weighting scheme is held constant throughout all experiments and is not tuned. 
All continuous prior scale parameters are sampled on the log scale. 
Specifically, the global style loading prior scale $\tau_\beta$ and the observation noise scale $\tau_\sigma$ are sampled with $\log_{10}$ values in $[-1,1]$, corresponding to a numerical range of approximately $[0.1,10]$. 
In addition, we introduce three group-specific style loading scales controlling count-based features, proportion-based features, and other advanced metrics, respectively; these group scales are sampled with $\log_{10}$ values in $[-0.7,0.7]$, corresponding to a numerical range of approximately $[0.2,5]$. 
In the first stage, all candidate configurations are evaluated using three-fold cross-validation with a reduced number of variational inference steps for computational efficiency. 
Within each fold, the model is fitted three times using different random seeds on the training split; for each fit, we compute the reconstruction mean squared error on the validation split, and convert the training-set style weight matrix into hard cluster assignments by taking the maximum component. 
Pairwise agreement between repeated fits is then computed and averaged to quantify the stability of the learned latent structure with respect to random initialization. 
Configurations are ranked primarily by this structural stability criterion, with reconstruction error used as a secondary criterion when stability is comparable. 
In the second stage, a small subset of configurations passing the first stage is trained once on the full training set for a short duration and evaluated using posterior predictive checks. 
Posterior predictive replicated data are generated from posterior means and compared with observed data in terms of feature-wise means, standard deviations, quantiles, cross-feature correlation structure, and value diversity; all posterior predictive tail probabilities are aggregated into a single calibration score defined as the average absolute deviation from $0.5$. 
To assess whether the model captures nontrivial joint structure, we further construct a null model via column-wise permutation of the observed data, compute the same statistics under the null, and define a structural separation score as the mean absolute difference between the model and null posterior predictive results. 
Only configurations that exhibit both reasonable calibration and clear separation from the null model are retained. 
In the final stage, the remaining configurations are evaluated using three-fold cross-validation with a larger number of training steps and repeated fits, and the final model is selected by prioritizing maximal structural stability followed by minimal validation reconstruction error.

\paragraph{Style Interpretation.}
Figure~\ref{fig:style-radar} displays radar plots of the posterior mean style prototypes, with all features standardized by z-score normalization.
Style~0 corresponds to low-usage players with limited aggregate impact, exhibiting below-average scoring and impact metrics despite moderate functional contributions such as assists or rebounds.
Style~1 is characterized by elevated efficiency, most notably a high WS/48, while maintaining low usage and modest box-score production, indicating contributions driven primarily by per-minute efficiency rather than volume.
Style~2 presents a rebound-focused interior-oriented profile, with strong rebounding and limited playmaking or scoring involvement, representing the most specialized archetype among the learned styles.
Style~3 captures high-impact per-minute contributors, combining strong efficiency and impact metrics (BPM, VORP, WS/48) with relatively limited playing time, highlighting that efficiency-adjusted impact does not necessarily coincide with high usage.
In contrast, Style~4 represents high-usage scorers whose statistical output is dominated by scoring volume and minutes played, with only moderate efficiency and impact measures.
Although these styles are broadly interpretable, notable overlap remains, particularly between low-usage Styles~0 and~1 and between impact-driven Styles~3 and~4, reflecting partial posterior degeneracy in the latent space.
Consistent with this observation, posterior predictive checks indicate over-smoothing and underestimation of heavy-tailed behavior, motivating the structured extensions introduced latter to encourage stronger separation between styles and to better disentangle usage-driven and efficiency-driven effects.

\paragraph{Domain performance and model limitations.}
Across multiple diagnostics, the model captures coarse-grained structure in NBA player statistics, with interpretable latent styles reflecting distinct co-occurrence patterns among usage, efficiency, and impact-related features.
Posterior reconstructions align well with observed values for moderate players, indicating adequate performance within the bulk of the data distribution.
However, posterior predictive checks reveal systematic underestimation of higher-order moments and tail probabilities, and case studies show that superstar-level performances are represented as extreme realizations of existing high-usage styles rather than as distinct archetypes.
As a result, stylistic overlap persists across efficiency- and impact-driven profiles, suggesting posterior degeneracy in the latent space.
These findings motivate structured extensions that disentangle usage intensity from efficiency and impact and introduce mechanisms to prevent style collapse when modeling exceptional performance.
