\section{Data and Metrics Overview}
\subsection{Dataset}
Season-level NBA player data covering demographics, full-season box score totals, and advanced metrics. Core totals include games, minutes, points, rebounds, assists, and shooting percentages; advanced metrics summarize win contribution and on-court impact.

\subsection{Advanced Metrics (Focus)}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{WS and WS/48}: Win Shares (WS) measure total wins a player adds, split into offensive (OWS) and defensive (DWS) parts. WS/48 scales WS by playing time, representing wins added if the player maintained the same efficiency over 48 minutes.
    \item \textbf{BPM and VORP}: Box Plus/Minus (BPM) estimates points per 100 possessions a player adds relative to average, using a regression over full box score stats. Value Over Replacement Player (VORP) shifts BPM by a constant baseline (around --2) to express net impact versus a replacement-level player.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data Processing}
\subsection{Cleaning}
Removed players who never appeared in a game. Filled numeric missing values with 0 and set missing \texttt{college} to \texttt{International}. To avoid overfitting in dimensionality reduction while keeping these metrics for comparison, advanced metric columns were dropped during PCA and UMAP. Per-game stats were excluded as they are deterministic from totals and introduce collinearity with different units; totals were retained for consistency.

\subsection{Feature Engineering and Selection}
Counted player production by college, kept the top 20 sources (including International) as one-hot categories, grouped the rest into \texttt{Others}; standardized draft year and one-hot encoded draft team. These categorical features performed poorly in PCA/UMAP, so the final reduction used only numeric core features: \texttt{games}, \texttt{minutes\_played}, \texttt{points}, \texttt{total\_rebounds}, \texttt{assists}, \texttt{field\_goal\_percentage}, \texttt{3\_point\_percentage}, \texttt{free\_throw\_percentage}.

\section{Dimensionality Reduction and EDA}
\subsection{PCA Results}
Plotted cumulative explained variance and component-wise variance bars. The first two principal components explain about 74\% of total variance: $PC_1$ captures overall counting volume (low weight on shooting accuracy), while $PC_2$ is driven almost entirely by three-point and free-throw percentages and is weakly or negatively related to the other stats. We present the numeric-only PCA version because including advanced metrics produced unstable structure.

\subsection{PCA Visualization Insights}
In 2D PCA space we color points by quartiles of WS, WS/48, BPM, and VORP (red = lowest quartile, dark green = highest) and highlight LeBron James, Tim Duncan, Kobe Bryant, Stephen Curry, and Kyrie Irving. Observations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Clear WS gradient}: Along $PC_1$ scores progress from weaker to stronger; larger $PC_1$ means bigger raw totals, and sparsity at the high end reflects superstar rarity.
    \item \textbf{WS/48 more diffuse}: Lowest quartile remains clustered, but middle and upper quartiles intermingle, showing that efficient low-usage players can still add wins; teams are not built solely by stacking high-usage stars.
    \item \textbf{BPM sensitive to shooting}: Unless $PC_1$ is extreme, stratification follows $PC_2$; high three-point and free-throw accuracy lifts BPM substantially.
    \item \textbf{VORP non-monotonicity}: Lowest quartile is not always at small $PC_1$; some high-volume players lag in VORP, while low-volume players are not always most harmful, highlighting replacement-value differences.
    \item \textbf{Star placement matches intuition}: James, Duncan, and Bryant lead on $PC_1$; Curry and Irving peak on $PC_2$. At equal $PC_2$, Curry sits higher on $PC_1$, consistent with high efficiency plus high output.
\end{itemize}
